## The 30-minute idea

Most bounty guides assume you have hours to spare. This one assumes you do not. You have a job, a commute, a family, or classes — and still want a shot at the prizes. The answer is not to work longer; it is to work the same short block every day, at the same time, with a fixed checklist. Thirty minutes a day beats a five-hour binge once a month, because bounties reward people who show up while the bounty is still open.

Pick your slot and defend it. Morning coffee, lunch break, or the last half hour before bed — any time works, as long as it is the same time every day. Put a daily alarm on your phone named "make.fun 30". When it rings, you open the site, run the checklist below, and close it when the timer ends.

## Step 2 — The daily 30 minutes

Set a timer for 30 minutes. Work through the blocks in order and stop when the timer rings, even mid-task. The routine only works if it ends on time.

### Minutes 0–5: Scan the board

Open https://make.fun/bounty/bring-the-humans-02 and then the open bounties list. Note three things: which bounties close soonest, which ones are new today, and which ones fit something you can actually build or write in the time you have. Ignore everything else. Five minutes of scanning beats an hour of drifting.

### Minutes 5–10: Pick one thing

Choose exactly one bounty to focus on today. One. If you are building, pick the bounty whose requirements you understand best. If you are inviting people, pick the bounty your referral link points to and plan one message about it. Write your choice down in a notes app: the bounty name, its deadline, and the single next step you will take on it today.

### Minutes 10–25: Do the one thing

This is the work block. Fifteen uninterrupted minutes on your single next step:

- **Building an entry:** read the requirements, then write the smallest version that satisfies them. A finished small entry beats an unfinished ambitious one.
- **Writing a guide:** draft one section, not the whole guide. One clear section a day becomes a complete guide in a week.
- **Inviting people:** send one honest message to one person who would genuinely like this — a friend who codes, a classmate who likes games, a coworker curious about AI. Tell them what make.fun is in your own words and share your link. Never spam groups, never buy signups, and never fake accounts: only verified real humans count, and fake signups get you nothing.

### Minutes 25–30: Log and close

Write three lines in your notes: what you did today, what the next step is tomorrow, and whether any bounty deadline moved. Check your referral count once — not five times. Then close the tab. The routine is done, and tomorrow you start from your notes instead of from zero.

## Step 3 — The weekly rhythm

A daily routine needs a weekly shape, or the days blur together. Here is the one I use:

- **Monday — Pick:** choose the bounty you will chase this week and read its rules properly.
- **Tuesday to Thursday — Build:** one work block a day on the entry, following the daily routine above.
- **Friday — Polish:** test what you built, fix the obvious problems, and re-read the requirements line by line.
- **Saturday — Invite:** this is your referral day. Share your link once, in one place where it belongs, with a sentence about why you use the platform.
- **Sunday — Rest or review:** either take the day off entirely, or spend your 30 minutes reviewing the leaderboard and planning next week. Rest is part of the system, not a failure of it.

## Common mistakes busy people make

- **Waiting for a free weekend.** Bounties close on their own schedule, not yours. Thirty minutes today is worth more than a free weekend that never comes.
- **Chasing every bounty.** Picking five bounties means finishing none. One bounty, one next step, one day at a time.
- **Checking the leaderboard all day.** It changes slowly and distracts constantly. Check it once, during minutes 25–30, and move on.
- **Skipping verification.** An unverified account — yours, or someone you invited — does not count. Finish setup properly on day one and help the people you invite finish theirs.
- **Quitting after a quiet week.** Referrals and entries compound. The person who shows up for thirty minutes a day for three weeks is ahead of the person who showed up once, brilliantly, and never came back.
